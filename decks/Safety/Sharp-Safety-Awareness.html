Sharp Safety Awareness

Preventing needlestick and sharp injuries in the Emergency & Trauma Department and during pre-hospital care.

It happened here · January to July 2026

Five of our colleagues were injured by sharps in seven months

Pattern

In 4 of 5 cases the injured person was assisting or handling the sharp after use, not performing the main procedure. Every one was preventable.

Risk map · tap an area

Every area of ETD has its own sharp risks

Risks

  • Lancets from glucometer checks left on the counter
  • Patients’ own sharps: insulin pens, needles from people who inject drugs, blades in pockets
  • High turnover, quick procedures done standing up

Controls

  • Single-use safety lancets, discarded into a bin at the triage counter
  • Ask before searching belongings: “Is there anything sharp in your pockets?” Never put a hand in blindly. Empty bags onto a tray
  • Small sharps bin at every triage station

Risks

  • High volume of blood taking and cannulation
  • Crowded cubicles, several staff around one tray
  • Learners (medical students) handling sharps
  • Paediatric patients who struggle

Controls

  • One-arm-reach set-up with a bin at every procedure
  • Tube racks and transfer devices on every trolley
  • Students supervised from preparation to disposal
  • Two-person rule for children. Roles agreed before starting

Risks

  • Many hands around one patient and time pressure
  • Multiple sharps at once: IO needles, arterial and central lines, chest drains, scalpels
  • Sharps lost in linen, under the patient or on the floor after resuscitation

Controls

  • Bin on the resus trolley within reach of each operator
  • Each operator disposes of their own sharps and says “sharp” out loud
  • Sharps sweep after every resuscitation, before the patient is transferred and before linen is changed

Risks

  • Suture needles, scalpels, local anaesthetic syringes
  • Glass or debris hidden in wounds
  • Splash during irrigation and incision & drainage

Controls

  • Handle suture needles with instruments, never fingers
  • Neutral zone (kidney dish) for passing sharps
  • Blade remover or safety scalpel
  • Eye protection for irrigation. Count sharps before closing the set

Risks

  • Fatigue and slower reactions. Incidents 3 and 4 happened at 0500H and 0425H
  • Fewer staff available to hold patients
  • Less supervision for learners
  • Delay in reporting: “I’ll tell someone in the morning”

Controls

  • Don’t take shortcuts when tired. Slow down for the sharp steps
  • Call for a holder rather than working alone
  • Report immediately to the sister on call
  • Defer non-urgent bloods if it is unsafe to take them now

Risks

  • Moving vehicle, braking and bumps
  • Confined space and poor lighting
  • Patients in pain or agitated, few hands to help
  • Roadside scenes: broken glass and debris

Controls

  • Cannulate before departure or with the vehicle stopped
  • Sharps bin mounted within reach
  • Safety cannulas only

SHARP SAFETY · ETD ORIENTATION
Pre-hospital care (PHC) · ambulance

The ambulance is the hardest place to handle a sharp safely

What makes it risky

  • Vehicle movement: braking, corners, potholes
  • Confined space. The assistant is often directly in the needle path
  • Poor lighting and noise
  • Patients in pain, confused, intoxicated or agitated
  • Only one or two crew to help
  • Sharps bin loose, full or out of reach
  • Scenes with broken glass, metal and debris
  • Long transfers and fatigue

How to minimise it

  • Cannulate before departure, or ask the driver to stop if IV access is needed on the way
  • Brace yourself; seat belt where possible. Secure the patient’s limb before the needle goes in
  • Give analgesia and explain first, so the patient is less likely to jerk
  • Use safety cannulas. Stylet goes into the mounted bin straight away
  • Assistant holds above the site, behind the needle tip
  • Gloves before patient contact; heavy-duty gloves at crash scenes
  • After handover: check stretcher, linen and floor for sharps. Restock the bin and cannulas
Incident 2

Good practice: the paramedic recognised the exposure, told the crew, and irrigated the wound as soon as they reached hospital. Report it on arrival.

Part 2

Safe technique

Golden rules, the one-hand technique, the one-arm-reach set-up, the sharps bin, and PPE.

02
Golden rules

Six rules for every sharp, every time

1

Never recap

If recapping is unavoidable (for example, a second local anaesthetic infiltration), use the one-hand scoop technique only.

2

You use it, you bin it

The person who used the sharp disposes of it, straight away, never later and never by someone else.

3

Bin within one arm’s reach

Place the bin before you start. If it is not within reach, don’t start.

4

Hands behind the tip

Yours and your assistant’s. Nobody’s fingers in front of, or in line with, the needle.

5

No hand-to-hand passing

Use a neutral zone such as a kidney dish, and say “sharp” or “passing” out loud.

6

Gloves on, injuries reported

Gloves for every procedure. Every injury is reported immediately, however small.

SHARP SAFETY · ETD ORIENTATION
One-hand technique

Only one hand goes near the sharp. The other stays out of the way

A. One-hand scoop recap

  1. Lay the cap flat on the tray
  2. Holding the syringe in one hand, slide the needle into the cap
  3. Tilt upright, then press the cap against the surface to secure it
  4. Your free hand stays behind your back or flat on the table

B. One-hand blood transfer

  1. Prefer a closed vacuum system, so no transfer is needed
  2. Using a syringe? Attach a blood transfer device
  3. Tubes stand in a rack. Press the device onto each tube with one hand
  4. Never hold a tube while a needle is near it

C. Hands behind the tip

  1. Anchor the vein with your thumb below the insertion site
  2. Never anchor above the site or in line with the needle
  3. The assistant stabilises above the elbow, not near the site
  4. On withdrawal, the needle moves away from everyone

SHARP SAFETY · ETD ORIENTATION
The sharps bin

Your last line of safe disposal: use it properly

At the point of use

Within one arm’s reach, before you open the needle.

Right size

Long items (stylets, IO needles) go in fully without force.

Close at the fill line

Seal at ¾ full. Never press contents down or shake the bin.

Never put a hand in

Never retrieve anything from a bin, even something dropped by mistake.

Stable and secured

On a bracket or flat surface. Mounted securely in the ambulance.

Temporary closure

Use it when moving the bin between bays. Lock it fully when full.

Sharps only

Gauze, gloves and wrappers take up space and hide needles.

Labelled

Area and date on the label. Dispose of it through the clinical waste process.

SHARP SAFETY · ETD ORIENTATION
Personal protection

PPE is the last barrier, so make sure it is there

Gloves, every time

For every blood taking, cannulation and suturing, including emergencies and children. A glove does not stop a needle, but it wipes off some of the blood as the needle passes through.

Double gloves

Consider for high-risk situations: agitated patients, trauma with glass or bone, known blood-borne virus, and procedures in the ambulance.

Eyes & face

Goggles or a face shield for wound irrigation, incision & drainage, arterial lines and patients who spit. Splash exposure counts as an injury too.

Hepatitis B immunity

Complete the vaccination course and know your anti-HBs level (≥10 mIU/mL means protected). Check with Klinik Warga if unsure.

Remember

Incident 3: the needle went straight through the glove. PPE reduces harm, but technique prevents the injury.

03
Procedure 1 · Venepuncture / blood taking

Blood taking

  1. Check & explain
  2. Set up within one arm’s reach
  3. Hand hygiene & gloves
  4. Position & tourniquet
  5. Clean & dry
  6. Anchor below, puncture
  7. Fill tubes in order of draw
  8. Tourniquet off, then withdraw
  9. Activate safety & dispose
  10. Finish & check

Check & explain

Confirm identity with two identifiers and check the request form. Explain the procedure and get consent. Ask about fainting or difficult veins.

Sharp safety: is the patient agitated, confused or a frightened child? Go to the special-situation steps before you open a needle.

Set up within one arm’s reach

Tray with a closed vacuum holder (or safety butterfly), tubes standing in a rack, tourniquet, swab, gauze and plaster. Open everything now.

Sharp safety: sharps bin on your dominant side, lid open, within reach. No bin within reach means you don’t start.

Hand hygiene & gloves

Perform hand hygiene and put on well-fitting gloves. Ask a colleague to help if the patient may move.

Incident 4: the needle went into an ungloved finger. Gloves are never optional, not even for “a quick one”.

Position & tourniquet

Support the arm, extended, on a firm surface with good light. Apply the tourniquet 7.5–10 cm above the site and select the vein.

Clean & dry

Clean with 2% chlorhexidine in 70% alcohol for about 30 seconds and let it dry completely. Do not touch the site again.

Anchor below, puncture

Your thumb pulls the skin taut 3–5 cm below the site. Insert bevel up at 15–30°.

Sharp safety: never anchor above the site or with a finger in line with the needle. Assistant holds above the elbow.

Fill tubes in order of draw

Steady the holder with one hand and push each tube in with the other.

Using a syringe? Transfer with a blood transfer device, tubes standing in the rack. Never hold a tube in your hand and never push a needle through the stopper (Incidents 1 & 3).

Tourniquet off, then withdraw

Release the tourniquet before removing the needle (within 1 minute of applying it). Place gauze over the site and withdraw smoothly.

Activate safety & dispose

Activate the safety mechanism with one hand and drop the whole unit (needle and holder) straight into the bin.

You used it, you bin it. Never recap, bend or unscrew the needle by hand, and never put it down on the tray.

Finish & check

Ask the patient to press on the gauze. Label tubes at the bedside. Remove gloves and perform hand hygiene.

Final sweep: look at the bed, tray and floor. No sharp left behind.

Procedure 2 · Peripheral IV cannulation

IV cannulation

  1. Indication & device
  2. Prepare everything first
  3. Hand hygiene, gloves, position
  4. Tourniquet, select, clean
  5. Anchor & insert
  6. Advance catheter
  7. Stylet straight to bin
  8. Connect, flush, secure
  9. If the attempt fails
  10. Finish & check

Indication & device

Confirm that IV access is needed now. Choose the smallest suitable gauge and a safety (passive) cannula. Identify the patient, explain and get consent.

Prepare everything first

Cannula, primed T-connector, saline flush, dressing, label, gauze, tourniquet and swab, plus the sharps bin within arm’s reach.

Incident 5: two staff reached into the same tray mid-procedure. Everything should be ready before the skin is punctured. One tray, one owner.

Hand hygiene, gloves, position

Arm on a firm surface with good light. Agree roles: the assistant stabilises the limb above the site, away from the needle.

Tourniquet, select, clean

Apply the tourniquet, choose a straight vein away from joints, clean with chlorhexidine-alcohol and let it dry.

Anchor & insert

Anchor below the site. Insert bevel up at 10–30°. When you see flashback, lower the angle and advance a further 1–2 mm.

Sharp safety: your free hand stays behind the tip throughout.

Advance catheter

Advance the catheter off the needle into the vein. Release the tourniquet. Press on the vein beyond the catheter tip to limit backflow.

Stylet straight to bin

Withdraw the stylet. The safety clip covers the tip. Drop it straight into the bin yourself.

Incident 2: the exposed stylet struck the assistant during withdrawal. Never put the stylet on the bed or tray, and never hand it to anyone.

Connect, flush, secure

Attach the primed T-connector, flush to confirm patency, secure with a transparent dressing, and label with the date and time.

If the attempt fails

First, stylet into the bin. Then press on the site and reassess. Use a new cannula for every attempt.

Never reinsert the stylet into the catheter (risk of catheter shear and injury). After two attempts, ask a more experienced colleague.

Finish & check

Remove gloves and perform hand hygiene. Document the site, gauge and number of attempts.

Final sweep: wrappers, caps and stylets accounted for before you leave the bay.

Procedure 3 · Wound toilet & suturing

Toilet & suturing

  1. Set up the field
  2. Protect yourself
  3. Local anaesthetic
  4. Irrigate & explore
  5. Load the needle with instruments
  6. Suture & tie
  7. Hands-free passing
  8. Scalpel safety
  9. Count & clear

Set up the field

Open the dressing set, then place the sharps bin at the point of use. Decide on a neutral zone (a kidney dish) where sharps are placed and picked up.

Protect yourself

Sterile gloves, apron, and eye protection or a face shield, because irrigation splashes.

Local anaesthetic

Draw up with a blunt fill needle if available, then change to the injection needle. Between infiltrations, put the syringe down in the neutral zone.

Recapping for a second infiltration? Use the one-hand scoop only.

Irrigate & explore

Irrigate under splash protection. Explore the wound for glass or debris with instruments under good light.

Never sweep a wound with your finger. Glass and bone fragments are sharps too.

Load the needle with instruments

Grasp the needle with the needle holder about two-thirds of the way back from the tip. Reposition it with toothed forceps.

Never touch a suture needle with your fingers, including when loading or retrieving it.

Suture & tie

Use instrument ties. Between bites, keep the needle clamped in the holder, pointing away from you and your assistant.

Hands-free passing

Pass needles, blades and syringes through the neutral zone only. Say “sharp” when you place one down.

Scalpel safety

Use a safety scalpel or a blade remover. If you have neither, use a needle holder to remove the blade, and dispose of it immediately.

Never remove or attach a blade with your fingers.

Count & clear

Count the needles and blades used. The operator disposes of every sharp before the set goes to the dirty utility.

Final sweep: drapes, linen, trolley and floor. Hidden needles injure cleaners and laundry staff.

Special situation · Aggressive or agitated adult

Blood taking & cannulation in an aggressive adult

  1. Pause: is it needed now?
  2. Make the area safe
  3. De-escalate
  4. Sedation per ED protocol
  5. Secure the limb
  6. Shortest needle time
  7. Stop rule
  8. Sweep, report, document

Pause: is it needed now?

If the sample or cannula is not urgent, defer it until the patient is calm. An unsafe procedure helps nobody.

Make the area safe

Call for help: security and enough trained staff. Remove unneeded equipment and sharps from reach. Keep a clear exit for staff.

PPE: gloves (consider double) and eye protection; agitated patients may spit.

De-escalate

One person talks. Use a calm voice and short sentences, explain what will happen, and offer choices. Involve family if it helps.

Sedation per ED protocol

If the patient is dangerous and treatment is needed, follow the ED sedation protocol. Often IM first (use a safety needle), then IV access once the patient is calm.

Secure the limb

Trained staff immobilise the limb above and below the joint. The operator starts only when the limb is still. Everyone else keeps hands away from the site.

Shortest needle time

The most experienced operator, a safety device, and the bin right there. Say “needle in” and “needle out” so the team knows.

Stop rule

If the patient moves suddenly: withdraw, activate safety, discard. Do not chase the vein with a needle in a moving limb.

Most injuries in struggling patients happen when the operator tries to finish the procedure rather than stop.

Sweep, report, document

Check the bed and floor for dropped sharps. Report any exposure immediately. Document restraint and sedation per policy.

Special situation · Struggling paediatric patient

Blood taking & cannulation in a struggling child

  1. Prepare child & parent
  2. Plan roles: two-person rule
  3. Everything ready
  4. Comfort positioning
  5. Gloves on, always
  6. Avoid open-needle techniques
  7. Stop rule
  8. Praise & sweep

Prepare child & parent

Explain in child-friendly words. Apply topical anaesthetic (e.g. EMLA) when time allows; consider sucrose for infants. Use distraction: a video, bubbles, toys.

For small volumes, consider whether capillary sampling is enough.

Plan roles: two-person rule

At least one holder and one operator, with a third person to fetch items. Brief everyone before starting.

Everything ready

Smallest suitable safety butterfly or safety cannula, tubes standing in the rack, primed T-connector, and the bin within arm’s reach.

Comfort positioning

Child on the parent’s lap chest-to-chest, or swaddle the infant. The trained holder stabilises the limb above and below the joint.

The parent comforts. Trained staff do the holding.

Gloves on, always

Even when you need to “feel” a small vein. Feel for the vein with gloves on.

Incident 4: a struggling child and an ungloved hand at 0425H.

Avoid open-needle techniques

Prefer a safety device. The ‘drop method’ keeps a bare needle near your fingers for a long time.

If it cannot be avoided: fingers well clear, bin right beside you, and needle out and disposed of before anything else.

Stop rule

If the child moves unpredictably: needle out, safety on, into the bin. Reposition and try again. After two attempts by one operator, escalate.

Praise & sweep

Comfort and praise the child. Check the bed, blanket and the parent’s lap for sharps and caps.

Part 4

If it happens

What to do in the first minutes and hours after a needlestick, sharp or splash injury.

04
Needlestick / sharp / splash injury · SASMEC flow

Wash. Report. Get assessed. Now.

1

Wash immediately

Under running tap water for about 10 minutes, with soap. Let it bleed freely. For a splash to the eyes or mouth, irrigate with water or saline.

2

Report to the site supervisor

Sister on duty or sister on call, straight away. Not at the end of the shift.

3

Report in i-Aduan

By you or the site supervisor.

4

Identify the source

Known: take the source’s blood (with consent) for HIV Ag/Ab, anti-HCV, HBsAg. Unknown: manage as an unknown source.

5

Get assessed

Go to Klinik Warga SASMEC @IIUM for assessment, incident investigation and immediate management.Go to ETD for immediate assessment, then to Klinik Warga on the next working day.

6

Follow-up

Infectious Disease Clinic (MOPD 2) by appointment. Complete every follow-up blood test.

ASAP

HIV post-exposure prophylaxis works best when started early, ideally within 2 hours and no later than 72 hours. Incident 3 was reported 9 hours late.

Don’t

  • Squeeze, scrub or suck the wound
  • Use bleach or strong antiseptics on the skin
  • Wait until the end of your shift
  • Feel embarrassed. Reporting protects you
