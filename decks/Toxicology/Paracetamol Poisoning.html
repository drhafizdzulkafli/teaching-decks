Paracetamol · acetaminophen · APAP

Safe at the dose, dangerous beyond it

Analgesic and antipyretic that acts mainly by inhibiting central prostaglandin synthesis. It is in almost every Malaysian home, often in combination products.

Adult max (oral)4 g/day1 g 6-hourly; lower (≈3 g) if frail, malnourished, alcohol use
Child dose15 mg/kgper dose, 4–6-hourly (max 60–75 mg/kg/day)
IV, adult ≥ 50 kg1 g6-hourly, max 4 g/day
IV, < 50 kg15 mg/kgmax 60 mg/kg/day
Formulations that change your management

Standard 500 mg tablet

Peak level 30–60 min. A strip of 10 = 5 g.

Soluble / fast-absorbing

Contain sodium bicarbonate. Absorbed faster; watch sodium load.

Modified-release 665 mg

Delayed, prolonged absorption. One 4-h level is not enough: repeat 4 h later.

Syrups & combinations

120 or 250 mg/5 ml syrups; with caffeine, codeine or orphenadrine. Ask about every product.

Potentially toxic

Acute single ingestion thresholds (Dart 2023, US/Canada): ≥ 150 mg/kg or ≥ 7.5 g (whichever is less) needs a timed level. Australian/NZ guidance uses ≥ 200 mg/kg or 10 g. > 30 g is a high-risk ingestion. The history is often wrong, so a timed level decides.

01
Part one

Why a safe drug turns toxic

Pharmacokinetics, the NAPQI story, and what it does to the liver, kidney and brain.

Pharmacokinetics

Fast in, mostly conjugated, briefly present

Absorption

30–60 min

Rapid from the small bowel. Peak 30 min (liquid), 45 min (tablet), 1–2 h or longer for modified-release. Delayed by opioids and anticholinergics.

Distribution

Vd ≈ 0.9 L/kg

10–30% protein bound. Crosses the blood–brain barrier and the placenta. Small Vd and low binding make it dialysable.

Metabolism

≈ 90% liver

Glucuronidation and sulfation to non-toxic conjugates. 5–10% by CYP2E1 (and 1A2, 3A4) to NAPQI.

Elimination

t½ ≈ 2–3 h

Conjugates excreted in urine; < 5% unchanged. Half-life is prolonged once the liver is injured (> 4 h is a bad sign).

Interactive · metabolism simulator

The NAPQI story

Hepatic glutathione 100%
Hepatocyte injury 0%

Glutathione stores fall below ≈ 30% before NAPQI starts to bind hepatocyte proteins. NAC refills glutathione, supplies sulfate and binds NAPQI directly.

Where the damage happens

Zone 3 first, then the rest of the body

Liver · centrilobular necrosis

Most CYP2E1, least oxygen

Zone 3 hepatocytes around the central vein make the most NAPQI and have the least oxygen and glutathione, so they die first. ALT can exceed 10 000 IU/L.

Kidney

Acute tubular necrosis

Renal CYP2E1 makes NAPQI locally. AKI can occur even without severe liver injury.

Mitochondria

Early lactic acidosis

Massive levels block mitochondrial respiration: acidosis and coma within hours, before liver failure.

Brain

Encephalopathy

Early: rare CNS depression in massive ingestion. Late: hepatic encephalopathy and cerebral oedema.

Pancreas · heart

Less common

Raised amylase or pancreatitis; myocardial injury is reported in severe poisoning.

Three ways to overdose

Higher-risk patients (lower thresholds in RSTI): chronic alcohol use, malnutrition or fasting, enzyme inducers (e.g. rifampicin, carbamazepine), low body weight, and children who are unwell or dehydrated.

02
Part two

The ED approach

Resuscitate, take a precise history, time the level, and let the clock decide.

Assessment

The first 10 minutes

A

Airway

Usually patent. Reduced GCS early? Think co-ingestant (opioid, sedative) or massive ingestion.

B

Breathing

Rapid deep breathing may be acidosis: massive paracetamol or salicylate.

C

Circulation

Vitals, IV access, bloods. Fluids for vomiting and dehydration.

D

Disability

GCS, pupils, bedside glucose: hypoglycaemia means liver failure or a co-ingestant.

E

Exposure & safety

Jaundice, RUQ tenderness, self-harm marks. Intentional? Keep the patient safe and supervised.

Investigations

What to send, and when

Serum PCMThe key test. At ≥ 4 h for a known acute ingestion; immediately if late, unknown time or unwell.
ALT / ASTBaseline, then to track injury and decide when to stop NAC.
PT / INRBest marker of liver synthetic function and prognosis.
RP · glucoseCreatinine for AKI; glucose for hypoglycaemia in liver failure.
VBG + lactateIf unwell or massive ingestion: early acidosis = parent-drug toxicity.
PhosphateA high phosphate at 48–96 h is a poor prognostic sign.
Every intentional overdose also gets
  • Serum salicylate
  • ECG: QRS, QTc (co-ingestants)
  • Capillary glucose
  • Pregnancy test in women of reproductive age
Units trap

mg/L = µg/mL. To convert to µmol/L, multiply by 6.62.
150 mg/L ≈ 1000 µmol/L. Check what your lab reports before you plot.

A level drawn before 4 hours cannot rule toxicity in or out (absorption is not complete). It only shows the drug is present.

03
Part three

Decontaminate, then NAC

Charcoal for the early arrival, the antidote for everyone who needs it, and knowing when to stop.

Gastrointestinal decontamination

Activated charcoal

50 g

Single dose, orally. Child: 1 g/kg (max 50 g). Most benefit within 1–2 h; reasonable up to 4 h, and later for MR tablets or co-ingestants.

Give if

  • Potentially toxic dose
  • Within 4 h of ingestion
  • Alert, cooperative, airway safe
  • Massive or MR ingestion even later

Do not give if

  • Reduced GCS without a protected airway
  • Vomiting, ileus or bowel obstruction
  • Corrosive co-ingestion
  • It would delay NAC

No role for gastric lavage or ipecac. Charcoal does not reduce the effect of IV NAC, so there is no need to delay either one.

The antidote

N-acetylcysteine: when and why

A glutathione precursor. Almost 100% effective within 8 hours, and still worth giving late, even in established liver failure.

1

Refills glutathione

Provides cysteine for glutathione synthesis.

2

Feeds sulfation

More drug goes down the safe sulfate pathway.

3

Binds NAPQI

Directly conjugates the toxic metabolite.

4

Late benefit

Antioxidant; improves microcirculation in liver failure.

Start NAC if any one applies
1Level on or above the treatment line (4–24 h after a known acute ingestion).
2Suspected > 150 mg/kg (or 7.5 g) and the level will not be back by 8 h after ingestion: start, then decide.
3Unknown time or staggered: level > 10 mg/L or raised ALT.
4RSTI: level ≥ 20 mg/L or raised ALT.
5Late presentation (> 24 h) with detectable level or any liver injury.
6High-risk: > 30 g, above the high-risk line, or early acidosis / altered mental state / shock.
Interactive · IV NAC dosing

Calculate the bags

60 kg

Ampoule strength 200 mg/mL. Dose by actual weight, capped at 100 kg. Children < 40 kg need reduced diluent volumes (hyponatraemia risk). The 2-bag regimen gives fewer anaphylactoid reactions. Always check your hospital protocol (MOH Pharmacy Handbook uses the 3-bag regimen). Oral NAC: 140 mg/kg, then 70 mg/kg 4-hourly.

During and after the infusion

Reactions, and when to stop

Anaphylactoid reaction · 10–20% with the 1-h loading bag

Rate-related histamine release, not true allergy. Flushing, itch, urticaria; less often bronchospasm, angioedema, hypotension. More common when the paracetamol level is low.

1Pause the infusion and assess ABC.
2Give an antihistamine (e.g. IV chlorphenamine); salbutamol for wheeze; adrenaline only for true anaphylaxis with shock.
3Restart once settled, at the same or a slower rate. A previous reaction is not a contraindication.
4Also: nausea and vomiting (give ondansetron), fluid overload and hyponatraemia in small children.
Interactive · can I stop NAC at the end of the regimen?

Do not stop by the clock alone. Check bloods near the end of the last bag (≈ 2 h before), so there is no gap in treatment.

Special situations

When the usual rules bend

> 30 g · above high-risk line

Massive

  • Early coma, lactic acidosis, shock: parent-drug toxicity
  • Start NAC immediately; increased dose (e.g. double the last bag) on toxicologist advice
  • Haemodialysis if level > 900 mg/L with altered mental state or acidosis (EXTRIP); increase NAC during dialysis
Fomepizole is used in some centres to block CYP2E1.
Modified-release

Two levels

  • Absorption is delayed and prolonged
  • Level at 4 h and 4 h later
  • Start NAC if either is above the line
  • Consider charcoal even beyond 4 h
Same for opioid or anticholinergic co-ingestion (slowed gut).
Pregnancy

Treat the mother

  • Same thresholds as non-pregnant patients
  • IV NAC preferred; it crosses the placenta
  • Fetal liver makes NAPQI late in pregnancy
  • Involve O&G early
Delay to NAC is the main risk to the fetus.
Children

Small and curious

  • Accidental liquid ingestion < 6 y: assess if ≥ 200 mg/kg
  • Faster glutathione synthesis: hepatotoxicity is rarer
  • Weight-based NAC with reduced fluid volumes
  • Look for neglect; give safety advice
Most toddlers with a sip of syrup can go home after assessment.
When it goes wrong

Acute liver failure & transplant

  • Continue NAC until recovery, transplant or death.
  • Treat glucose, electrolytes, acidosis and infection; ICU care. FFP only for bleeding or procedures: the INR is your prognostic marker.
  • Discuss early with the hepatology / liver transplant team (national centre: Hospital Selayang).

Disposition & prevention

Medically fit

  • NAC completed, stopping criteria met
  • Or level below the line at 4–8 h with normal ALT
  • Clear written advice: return with vomiting, RUQ pain, jaundice, drowsiness

Mind as well as liver

  • Every intentional overdose: psychiatric assessment before discharge
  • Ask about suicidal intent, plan and supports
  • Under-18s: involve family and child-protection services when needed

Prevention

  • Keep medicines locked and out of children’s reach
  • Use the dosing syringe; do not mix two paracetamol products
  • Smaller pack sizes at home; return unused medicines to the pharmacy
Summary

Six things to remember

01

Well ≠ safe

Stage I patients look well. Labs are normal for the first day. Do not wait for symptoms.

02

Do the maths

≥ 150 mg/kg or 7.5 g acutely is potentially toxic. > 30 g is high risk.

03

NAPQI is the villain

Conjugation saturates, glutathione runs out, NAPQI kills zone 3 hepatocytes.

04

Time the level

Nomogram only for a single acute ingestion with known time, 4–24 h. Not for staggered or RSTI.

05

NAC before 8 h

If the level will not be back by 8 h, start NAC first. Calculate carefully, cap at 100 kg.

06

Stop on criteria

Level < 10 mg/L, ALT improving, INR < 2, patient well. Escalate liver failure early.
