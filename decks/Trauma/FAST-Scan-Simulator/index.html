Where FAST fits

FAST is performed during the primary survey, as an adjunct to C – Circulation with haemorrhage control. It answers one question: is there free fluid (presumed blood) in the peritoneum or pericardium?

Key numbers

  • Sensitivity for free intraperitoneal fluid about 71–74%; specificity 95–98% (AIUM).
  • Roughly 200 mL or more of intraperitoneal fluid is usually needed before it is visible; a negative FAST does not exclude small bleeds.
  • Probe: curvilinear (2–5 MHz); a phased-array (cardiac) probe also works.
  • No absolute contraindications — but FAST must never delay resuscitation or transfer to theatre.

What FAST cannot tell you

  • Retroperitoneal bleeding (pelvic fracture, renal, aortic) — FAST can be negative in a shocked patient.
  • Solid-organ injury without haemoperitoneum, hollow-viscus or diaphragm injury.
  • The type of fluid — ascites, urine, peritoneal dialysis fluid and physiological pelvic fluid all look black.
  • A single snapshot — bleeding evolves. Repeat FAST whenever the patient's condition changes.

Common pitfalls

  • Over-reliance on ultrasound; delaying the transfer to theatre.
  • Not scanning the inferior pole of the kidney / caudal liver tip.
  • Missing clotted blood — it is grey (isoechoic), not black.
  • Empty bladder (catheter already in) — no acoustic window for the pelvis.
  • Mimics: perinephric fat, gallbladder, IVC, stomach/bowel fluid, seminal vesicles, ovarian cyst, epicardial fat pad.

Fluid or not? A quick rule

Free fluid (blood)

Anechoic (black), sharp angular edges that fill the gaps between organs, sits in the dependent spaces, no wall of its own. Fresh blood is black; clot is grey and lumpy.

Normal structures that are also black

Bladder, gallbladder, IVC/vessels, heart chambers — all have a smooth wall, a predictable shape and position, and are contained.

Grey but not fluid

Perinephric fat (echogenic, often bilateral), epicardial fat pad (anterior only, moves with the heart), bowel (peristalsis, gas shadows).

Make the scan easier

  • Rib shadows? Rotate the probe slightly oblique to lie between the ribs, or ask for a deep breath.
  • Slight Trendelenburg increases fluid in the RUQ/LUQ; reverse Trendelenburg increases it in the pelvis.
  • Turn the depth up for the heart (16–20 cm); lower the gain behind the bladder (posterior enhancement).
